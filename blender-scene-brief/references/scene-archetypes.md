# Scene archetypes

Seeds, not templates. Find the closest archetype, take its habits of thought — what counts as
a hidden zone here, what the rubric should weight, which shortcut this scene invites — then
write the actual spec from the user's scene. Never paste these phrases into a brief.

Each entry gives: **scope metric** · **zones you will forget** · **rubric bias** · **the
shortcut this scene invites**.

---

## 1. Hospitality interior — restaurant, bar, café, hotel lobby
Scope: floor area + covers or keys. Forgotten zones: back-of-house prep, dishwash, waste
holding, staff route, service lift, storage, restrooms. Rubric bias: operational authenticity
and lighting carry the most weight; planning and circulation next. Invited shortcut: a room of
identical tables with a pretty window, and a decorative kitchen nobody could cook in.

## 2. Retail — shop, market stall, supermarket, showroom
Scope: floor area + SKU or fixture count. Forgotten zones: stockroom, goods-in, fitting rooms,
staff area, till back-of-counter, security and signage systems. Rubric bias: merchandising
density and stock credibility; wayfinding and sightlines. Invited shortcut: shelves as a
repeating texture with no individual product, price labels, or gaps where things sold.

## 3. Workshop and industrial — garage, factory floor, forge, laboratory
Scope: floor area + station or machine count. Forgotten zones: waste and fluid store,
wash-down, compressed air and extraction, spares racking, mezzanine, yard. Rubric bias:
tooling authenticity and process logic dominate; construction detail next. Invited shortcut:
tools laid out decoratively, and clean floors under machines that would have stained them.

## 4. Domestic interior — apartment, family house, shared flat, cabin
Scope: floor area + rooms or occupants. Forgotten zones: utility, storage, circulation and
landings, the balcony, the bit behind the door. Rubric bias: occupancy evidence and material
honesty; light quality through real windows. Invited shortcut: a catalogue interior with no
sign that anyone lives there, and identical furniture in every room.

## 5. Transport — station, airport gate, ferry terminal, depot, carriage interior
Scope: platform or gate count + linear metres. Forgotten zones: staff rooms, plant, ticketing
back office, cleaning stores, tunnel or apron edge, level changes and step-free routes.
Rubric bias: wayfinding and passenger flow; systems and services detail. Invited shortcut:
signage as the only proof of function, and a concourse with no ceiling coordination.

## 6. Cultural and heritage — temple, museum gallery, library, theatre, ruin
Scope: floor area + a period or collection metric. Forgotten zones: back rooms, conservation
and storage, staff circulation, plant, the roof structure above the visible ceiling. Rubric
bias: construction and ornament authenticity carries the most; lighting and atmosphere next.
Invited shortcut: ornament as a repeated decal, and a period that is a mixture of three.

## 7. Exterior and landscape — street, courtyard, farmyard, campsite, dockside
Scope: site area + a frontage or unit count. Forgotten zones: drainage, service yards, refuse,
utilities, boundaries and thresholds, what is across the street. Rubric bias: environmental
context depth and weather response; ground and terrain credibility. Invited shortcut: a flat
ground plane with props on it and a backdrop that is visibly a card.

## 8. Vehicle and vessel interior — cockpit, bridge, cab, submarine, spacecraft
Scope: internal volume + crew stations. Forgotten zones: equipment bays, access panels, stowage,
the space behind the panel, egress routes. Rubric bias: systems legibility and control
authenticity dominate; ergonomics next. Invited shortcut: greebles instead of systems, and
displays that do not correspond to anything.

## 9. Workplace — office floor, newsroom, control room, clinic
Scope: floor area + desks or beds. Forgotten zones: comms room, printers and supplies, break
area, cleaning store, meeting-room AV, cable management. Rubric bias: occupancy evidence and
equipment authenticity; daylight and glare handling. Invited shortcut: rows of identical
desks, all screens off, no cables and no personal traces.

## 10. Performance and leisure — club, arena, cinema, gym, pool
Scope: floor area + capacity. Forgotten zones: back of house, dressing rooms, plant, bars and
stores, technical galleries, safety and egress. Rubric bias: lighting design and atmosphere
carry the most; crowd-scale planning next. Invited shortcut: haze and coloured light standing
in for geometry that was never built.

## Sizing a scene you have not seen before

1. **Name the activity.** Everything else — the zones, the equipment, the critics — comes from
   what people actually do here, not from what the room looks like.
2. **Follow the thing that moves.** A dish, a motorcycle, a passenger, a parcel, a body. Trace
   its whole route; every place it stops is a zone, and the route itself becomes an adjacency
   rule.
3. **Find the dirty half.** Every scene has a public face and an operational back. If the
   zone list is all public, half the brief is missing.
4. **Ask what the domain expert would notice.** That question yields the rubric weighting, the
   critic roster, and the best scene-specific failures at once.
5. **Cameras last.** Once the zones are settled, the camera set falls out: one per zone, plus
   hero, plus a reverse audit, plus detail.
